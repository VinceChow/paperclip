## Part 1. X in 10 minutes (glossary for beginners)

| Term | What it means | Why you care |
|---|---|---|
| **Post** | A message of up to 280 characters (longer on paid plans) with optional images, video or a poll | Your basic unit |
| **Reply** | A post under someone else's post | Your #1 growth tool when you're new |
| **Repost / Quote** | Sharing someone's post; *quote* adds your own comment on top | Quotes put your take in front of your followers |
| **Thread** | Several posts chained together (reply to your own post) | Best for stories and step-by-step lessons |
| **For You** | The algorithm feed: posts X thinks a person will like, including from people they don't follow | Where new followers find you |
| **Following** | A time-ordered feed of accounts a person follows | Where existing followers see you |
| **Bookmark** | A private save | A strong signal of value to the algorithm |
| **Communities** | Topic groups (for example "Build in Public") that anyone can post into | Reach an interested crowd with zero followers |
| **Lists** | Your own curated feeds of accounts | Your daily "who to reply to" list |
| **Spaces** | Live audio rooms | Good for AMAs and demo days later |
| **Pinned post** | The post fixed at the top of your profile | Your permanent introduction |
| **Premium (blue check)** | Paid subscription | Reply visibility and reach; see Part 2 |
| **Impressions** | How many times a post was shown | Reach |
| **Engagement rate** | Interactions ÷ impressions | Quality of your content |

### How the For You feed ranks posts (2026, simplified)

X has published much of its ranking code, and third-party analyses agree on the direction, though not on exact numbers ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/twitter-algorithm/), [PostEverywhere source-code breakdown](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-x-twitter-algorithm-works), [Postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026)):

- **Conversations beat likes.** A reply, especially one *you* reply back to, is worth many times a like. Reposts, profile clicks and bookmarks also count far more than likes.
- **Replies are judged on substance.** Since early 2026, thoughtful replies rank above empty ones ("great post!").
- **Links cost reach.** Posts with an external link in the main text get noticeably less initial reach (estimates range from 30% to over 50%).
- **Consistency in one topic helps.** X groups accounts by topic. Posting about one theme helps it find the right audience for you.
- **Premium accounts get a boost,** most visibly in reply ranking.
- **Freshness matters.** Most of a post's reach happens in the first hours.

**What this means for you:** post about one clear theme, start conversations and answer every reply, keep links out of the main post, and show up daily.

## Part 2. Set up your account (day 1, about 90 minutes)

### 2.1 Personal account, not a brand account

People follow people, not startups. Use **your own name**. Later you can create `@[Brand]` for product announcements, but your personal account does the building in public.

### 2.2 Settings checklist

- [ ] **Username (@handle):** your real name, or name + a short word (`@mayabuilds`, `@alexsolo`). Avoid numbers and underscores if you can. Keep it under 15 characters.
- [ ] **Display name:** `[First Last] · building [Brand]` (update once the name is public). Before the name is chosen: `[First Last] · building for one-person companies`.
- [ ] **Profile photo:** a clear, friendly face photo with a plain background. No logo.
- [ ] **Header image (1500×500):** one line of text, "Building the platform for one-person companies, in public," plus a small progress marker such as "Day 1 → launch Jan 2027." Update it as you go.
- [ ] **Bio (160 characters).** Pick one:
  - "Building [Brand]: an AI team for one-person companies. Sharing everything: numbers, mistakes, lessons. Ex-[your past role]."
  - "Running a one-person company, building the platform for them. Weekly honest reports. Day [N]."
- [ ] **Location:** your city (it builds trust).
- [ ] **Website:** your waitlist page once live (marketing plan §4).
- [ ] **Security:** turn on two-factor authentication with an authenticator app. Don't use SMS.
- [ ] **Notifications:** turn on notifications for replies and mentions only, so you can answer quickly without constant buzzing.
- [ ] **Privacy:** keep your account public. Consider turning off precise location on posts.

### 2.3 Should you pay for Premium?

| Tier (2026, web prices; they vary by country) | What you get | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Basic, ~$3/month | Edit posts, longer posts, some reply boost, no checkmark | Skip |
| **Premium, ~$8/month** | Blue check, bigger reply boost, longer posts, reduced link penalty, eligibility for creator programs | **Yes, from week 2.** Your early growth comes from replies, and reply ranking favors Premium accounts. |
| Premium+, ~$40/month | Articles, no ads, largest reply boost | Not yet. Consider it after launch if long-form Articles become part of your routine. |

Sources: [Postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-premium-worth-it), [Startupik](https://startupik.com/x-premium-in-2026-full-pricing-and-feature-breakdown/). X changes these tiers often, so check the current page before paying.

### 2.4 Pinned post

Pin a short introduction on day 1 (template #1 in Part 11) and replace it with something stronger as you go: a milestone thread, then your launch post.

## Part 3. Find your people before you post much (days 1–3)

You're writing for **two audiences**:

1. **Solo business owners:** consultants, coaches, creators, designers, freelancers. They are your future customers.
2. **Builders and founders:** indie hackers, AI builders, build-in-public people. They are your early amplifiers, testers and peers.

### 3.1 Build three private lists

| List | Who goes on it | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily 25** | Mid-sized accounts (5K–100K followers) that post daily about solo business, AI tools or building in public, and whose posts get real replies | 25 |
| **Customers** | Real consultants, coaches, creators and designers who post about their business | 50–100 |
| **Peers** | Other founders at your stage (under 5K followers) building in public | 30–50 |

**How to find them:**
- Search for `"solopreneur"`, `"one person business"`, `"freelance"`, `"coaching business"`, `"build in public"`, `"indie hacker"` and `"AI agents"`. Switch to the **Latest** tab and look at who gets good replies.
- Look at who replies well under the big accounts.
- Join the **Build in Public** community and one or two communities for solopreneurs or freelancers.

### 3.2 Follow 100–200 relevant accounts

Only follow accounts in your topics. This teaches the algorithm what you're about, and it makes your For You feed a source of reply opportunities.

## Part 4. Your story and positioning

Write this down once and reuse it everywhere.

- **Who you are:** "I'm [you], a one-person company building the platform for one-person companies."
- **The problem you've seen:** solo owners lose leads, chase invoices and burn out on busywork. AI tools promise "AI employees" but sell credits and hype.
- **What you believe:** AI should *finish* real work, ask before anything important goes out, and be honest about what didn't work.
- **What you're doing:** building [Brand] in public, and running your own company on it.
- **What you'll share:** real numbers, decisions, mistakes, research and every weekly report.
- **What you won't do:** income claims, fake screenshots, "earn while you sleep."

**Your recurring series** (people follow series, and series make posting easy):

| Series | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Friday Report** | Every Friday | Your weekly results: waitlist, interviews, what shipped, what failed, what's next |
| **OPC Data** | 1–2× a week | A stat plus your take, from the research (Census data, lead-response studies) |
| **Build Log** | 2–3× a week | A screenshot or short screen recording of something you built, plus the decision behind it |
| **Customer Voice** | 1× a week | A lesson from an interview (anonymized and with permission) |
| **Ask** | 1× a week | A question or poll to your audience |

## Part 5. What to post: five content pillars

These fit the marketing plan's pillars (§6.2), tuned for X.

| Pillar | Share | Examples from our actual work |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Progress and numbers** | 30% | "Week 3: 212 waitlist, 11 interviews, 0 lines of product shipped. Here's why." · Friday Report |
| **2. Lessons and research** | 25% | "I read 539 one-star reviews of AI 'employee' apps. The #1 complaint isn't the AI." · "Small businesses take 42 hours on average to answer a lead." |
| **3. Behind the build** | 20% | Screenshots of the "Needs you" inbox, the "Done means…" card, the X-ray; decisions like "why no credits" |
| **4. Customer stories and questions** | 15% | Interview insights; polls ("What would you hand off first?") |
| **5. Personal and values** | 10% | Why a one-person company, bad days, what you refuse to do |

### 5.1 Post formats that work for new accounts

| Format | Why it works | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Short text post** (1–4 lines) | Fast to read, easy to reply to | End with a question or a clear opinion |
| **Screenshot + one line** | Shows real work, creates curiosity | Crop tightly; blur private data |
| **Short screen recording** (10–30 s) | Short, unpolished founder video performs best in 2026 ([Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/founder-build-in-public-content-playbook-2026)) | Film on your phone or record your screen; no fancy editing |
| **Thread** (5–8 posts) | Tells a story, earns bookmarks | First post must work on its own; last post asks a question |
| **Poll** | Easy engagement, real research | Use for customer discovery |
| **Chart or number card** | Data travels | Show your source |

### 5.2 How to write a post that gets read

1. **Hook in the first line.** A number, a surprise, a strong opinion, or a question. Example: "I read 539 angry reviews of AI business tools."
2. **One idea per post.**
3. **Short lines and white space.** Most people read on phones.
4. **Specific beats general.** "3 of 11 interviewees chase invoices every Sunday night" beats "people hate admin."
5. **End with an invitation.** "What would you add?" or "Which would you pick?"
6. **Links go in a reply**, not the main post. If a link must be in the main post, expect less reach.
7. **Hashtags:** zero or one. They look spammy on X in 2026.
8. **Read it out loud before posting.** If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it.

## Part 6. Grow with replies (your main tool for months 1–2)

With zero followers, your own posts reach almost nobody. **Replies put you in front of other people's audiences.** Recent founder playbooks recommend about 20 good replies a day plus a few original posts ([Innmind](https://blog.innmind.com/x-growth-playbook-for-startup-founders-2026/), [FounderDistro](https://www.founderdistro.com/blog/build-in-public-x-twitter-guide)).

### 6.1 The reply routine

- Open your **Daily 25** list twice a day.
- Reply **early:** within the first 30–60 minutes of their post.
- Write replies that **add something:**
  - **A data point:** "Related: small businesses answer only ~38% of inbound calls."
  - **A personal experience:** "I tried this with 5 coaches last week; 4 said the same thing."
  - **A respectful counterpoint:** "Agree on X, but for solo owners Y matters more because…"
  - **A good question:** "How do you decide what to automate first?"
- Never write "Great post!", never drop your link, and never mention your product unless someone asks.

### 6.2 Reply quality ladder

| Level | Example | Value |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Empty | "So true 🔥" | Hurts you |
| ⚠️ Agreeing | "Totally agree with this." | Near zero |
| ✅ Adding | "Agree. In my interviews, freelancers said the worst part is chasing invoices, not sending them." | Good |
| ⭐ Mini-post | 2–3 lines with a number, a story or a new angle that stands on its own | Best: earns profile clicks and follows |

### 6.3 Replying to your own replies

When someone replies to you, **always answer within a few hours**, ideally within the first hour. Conversation depth is one of the strongest ranking signals, and it's how acquaintances become followers.

### 6.4 Direct messages

- Use DMs to build relationships, never to pitch cold.
- Good reasons to DM: thank someone who shared your post; ask a customer-voice follow-up question ("Could I ask you 3 questions about how you handle invoices?"); congratulate a peer on a launch.
- Once you have a real relationship, ask for interviews (marketing plan §4.4) and pilot users.

## Part 7. Build-in-public rules: what to share and what not to

| Share ✅ | Don't share ❌ |
|---|---|
| Waitlist, interview and pilot numbers | Customers' names or data without written permission |
| What you built and why | Security details, keys, internal URLs |
| Mistakes and what you changed | Criticism of named competitors' customers or staff |
| Research with sources | Unverified or cherry-picked numbers presented as fact |
| Costs (for example "our AI cost per active user is ~$31/month") | Income claims or "you can make $X with this" |
| Decisions and trade-offs | Anything under NDA, or investor terms before they're public |

**Honesty rule:** when a number is bad, post it anyway, with what you're doing about it. It's the core of the brand ("we tell you what didn't work").

## Part 8. Measure what matters

Check **X Analytics** (Premium) once a week, on Friday, for 15 minutes. Don't check it daily; it distorts behavior.

| Metric | Early target (first 90 days) | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Followers | 500 by day 60 · 1,500–3,000 by launch | Reach is building |
| Replies received per post (median) | 3+ | People want to talk to you |
| Profile visits → follows | ≥ 5% | Your profile and pinned post convert |
| Bookmarks per thread | 10+ | Your content is useful |
| **Waitlist sign-ups from X** (tracked with UTM `?utm_source=x`) | 30% of your waitlist | X is doing its job |
| DMs from potential customers | 5+/week by month 2 | Real demand |

Keep a simple spreadsheet: date, post, format, impressions, replies, bookmarks, follows, sign-ups. Every Friday, write down one thing that worked and one to stop.

**Realistic expectations:** most accounts grow slowly for 4–8 weeks and then speed up. Your first 100 followers are the hardest. One reply to a big account can bring more followers than a week of your own posts.

## Part 9. Your daily routine (about 60–75 minutes)

| Time | Task | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Open the **Daily 25** list → write 8–10 good replies | 25 |
| Morning | Publish your post of the day (drafted the night before) | 5 |
| Midday | Answer every reply to your posts; 5 more replies on the Customers list | 15 |
| Evening | 5–7 replies on the Peers list and communities; answer DMs | 15 |
| Evening | Draft tomorrow's post (use Part 11) | 10 |

**Weekly:**
- **Monday:** plan the week's 5–7 posts.
- **Wednesday:** one thread.
- **Friday:** the Friday Report plus 15 minutes of analytics.
- **Sunday:** rest, or draft only; don't scroll.

**Posting times:** begin with 7–9 a.m. and 12–1 p.m. in the time zone of most of your audience (US Eastern if you target the US). After 4 weeks, use your own analytics.

**Tools (optional):**
- The native X scheduler (Premium) or a scheduler such as Typefully or Buffer for drafts.
- A notes app for ideas.
- Your phone for short videos.
- Later, the [Brand] Creator kit itself for repurposing (dogfooding, marketing plan §6.3).

## Part 12. Rules, safety and etiquette

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **No income or earnings claims**, ever: no "make $X," no "passive income." | The FTC actively pursues "AI business opportunity" income claims (market research §2.3) |
| **Disclose relationships.** If you post about a partner, sponsor or someone who paid you, say so (#ad or "paid partnership"). | FTC Endorsement Guides |
| **Real reviews and testimonials only**, with permission. No fake or AI-written testimonials. | FTC reviews rule (penalties up to $51,744 per violation) |
| **Label AI-generated images or video** when they look realistic. Don't use fake human presenters. | Platform labels; New York synthetic-performer law (since 9 June 2026) |
| **Don't use engagement bots, follow-for-follow schemes, bought followers or automated DMs.** | Against X's rules; can get the account limited |
| **Don't post customer data** or screenshots with names, emails or amounts without written permission. Blur by default. | Privacy and trust |
| **Criticize products, not people.** Don't pile on competitors or their customers. | Reputation; comparative-advertising accuracy |
| **Keep company secrets private:** keys, internal URLs, investor terms. | Security |
| **Take breaks.** Mute words or accounts that drain you; don't argue with trolls. Block and move on. | Sustainability |

### Common beginner mistakes

1. Posting links in every post (reach drops).
2. Posting only about the product (people follow people and stories).
3. Changing topics every day (the algorithm can't place you).
4. Chasing viral posts instead of conversations.
5. Checking analytics hourly.
6. Waiting to be "ready" before posting. Start with replies today.
7. Only talking to other founders. Remember, customers are solo business owners.
8. Quitting at week 4. Growth usually starts between weeks 6 and 10.
