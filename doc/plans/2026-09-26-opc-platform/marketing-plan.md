## 1. Goals, KPIs and unit economics

### 1.1 Targets by phase (base case; conservative and stretch in brackets)

| Phase | Window | Targets |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | W−16 → W−9 (Oct–Nov 2026) | Brand chosen and cleared · site v1 live · **1,500** waitlist · 30 interviews · 150 community members |
| Pilot | W−8 → W−3 (Dec 2026) | 15 paying concierge pilot users · ≥60% first-pass acceptance · 5 documented case studies · **4,000** waitlist |
| Pre-launch | W−2 → W−1 (Jan 2027) | **6,000** waitlist [3K / 10K] · 100 founding members (annual pre-pay) · 20 creators and affiliates signed · 3 embargoed press or newsletter features |
| Launch month | W0 → W+4 | 1,500 trials [700 / 3,000] · **300 paying** [150 / 600] · activation ≥ 50% |
| Month 6 | W+24 | **1,800 paying** [900 / 4,000] · CAC ≤ $200 · churn ≤ 4%/mo |
| Month 12 | W+48 | **4,000 paying** [1,500 / 10,000] · ~$260K MRR · second and third kit launches done |

**Sanity check against comparables:**
- Sintra reached ~40K paying customers in 12 months with aggressive promotions and affiliates paying up to 50% commission ([ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/sintra/sintra-arr-hit-12m-in-year-one-started-as-a-weekend-project), [Sintra affiliate](https://sintra.ai/affiliate)).
- Polsia reports ~7.6K customers in ~5 months.
- Lovable hit $17M ARR three months after its commercial launch ([Startup Riders](https://www.startupriders.com/p/how-lovable-hit-400m-arr-in-14-months)).
- Our base case is deliberately below these, because we lead with trust rather than promotions.

### 1.2 Unit economics that set channel limits

| Input | Assumption | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Blended ARPU | ~$65/mo (40% Solo, 50% Company, 10% Company+, some annual discounts) | Pricing in main report §6.3 |
| Gross margin | ~50% | Inference ≈ $31–39 per *active* user (harness doc §6.3); lighter users cost less |
| Monthly logo churn | 4% target (healthy SMB SaaS is 3–5%; Recurly B2B average 3.5%) | [EverHelp](https://www.ever-help.com/blog/saas-retention-rate-benchmarks), [Livmo](https://livmo.com/blog/saas-churn-benchmarks-valuation/) |
| Gross-profit lifetime value | $65 × 50% ÷ 4% ≈ **$810** | |
| **CAC ceiling** | LTV:CAC ≥ 3 → **≤ $270**; payback ≤ 8 months → **≤ $260** | Blended CAC target **≤ $200**; any single paid channel is capped at $250 |
| Cost of a trial | ~$3–8 of inference per trial with a capped allowance (for example 30 jobs) | Must be counted in CAC: at 15% trial-to-paid, that's ≈ $20–55 per paying customer |

**Worked example (Meta paid):**
- CPC $1.50 → visit-to-trial 6% → trial-to-paid 15% gives an ad CAC of **$167**.
- Add trial inference (~$33 per payer) for a total of **≈ $200**, which is right at target.
- So paid only works once landing conversion is ≥ 6% and trial-to-paid is ≥ 15%. That is why paid is gated in §12.

## 2. Positioning and messaging

### 2.1 Positioning statement
> **For** independent professionals who run a one-person company (consultants, coaches, creators, designers and marketers)
> **who** are the CEO, marketer, salesperson and admin all at once,
> **[Brand] is** the one-person-company platform
> **that** gives you a ready-made AI team for your industry that finishes real work to a clear definition of done, and asks you before anything goes out.
> **Unlike** AI "employees" that just draft, or "autonomous company" tools that take a cut of your revenue,
> **we** show you what got done, what it cost, and what didn't work, every week, at one flat price.

### 2.2 Messaging house

| Level | Message | Proof points |
|---|---|---|
| **Umbrella** | Run your one-person company like a team of ten. Approve what matters. | Kits give you a team on day one; the weekly report shows what got done |
| **Pillar 1: Outcomes you can check** | Every job has a visible definition of done. It isn't done until it passes. | Outcome contracts, independent checks, first-pass acceptance published per kit |
| **Pillar 2: You stay in control** | Nothing goes out without your OK, until *you* decide it can. | "Needs you" inbox, a trust ladder per action, one-tap mobile approvals, full history |
| **Pillar 3: Built for your business** | Kits for consultants, coaches, creators and designers, with property agents next. | Industry workflows (proposals, launches, repurposing, listing launches), not generic "AI employees" |
| **Pillar 4: Honest pricing** | One flat price. No revenue share. No token math. | $29 / $79 / $199; allowance shown in jobs and dollars; export everything |

### 2.3 Language rules (brand, legal and trust)

| ✅ Say | ❌ Never say | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "your team," "done," "approve," "one-person company," "company of one" | "passive income," "earn while you sleep," "guaranteed clients/revenue" | FTC Operation AI Comply targets AI "business opportunity" income claims |
| "AI teammates," "kits," "jobs" | "zero-human," "fully autonomous," "replace your employees" | Trust gap: 78% of small-business owners don't fully trust AI to work without oversight |
| "saves hours on X" (only when measured with pilot users) | Unmeasured numbers ("10x your revenue") | FTC substantiation; our honesty brand |
| "AI-drafted, approved by you" | Anything implying a human wrote AI content | EU AI Act Article 50; platform AI labels |
| Plain words: "definition of done," "checks," "your OK" | "agents," "orchestration," "heartbeats," "LLM," "tokens" (outside technical channels) | The audience isn't AI-literate |

### 2.4 Competitive one-liners (for sales pages, comparisons and replies)

| Alternative | Line |
|---|---|

## 3. Brand foundations

- **Naming.** Shortlist 3 names that are distinctive, 2–3 syllables, easy to say, and not descriptive.
  - Run knock-out searches at the USPTO and EUIPO in classes 9 and 42, and check "OPC" conflicts (the OPC Foundation holds registered marks).
  - Secure a `.com` (or `get[brand].com`) plus `.ai`, and matching handles on LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, X, Threads and Reddit.
  - Use: `[Brand]: the one-person company platform`.
- **Category assets.**
  - An "OPC" wordmark badge ("A One-Person Company, run on [Brand]") that users can put on their site or email signature. This is a share loop.
  - A definition we publish and repeat: *"A one-person company is a business run by one owner, powered by a team of software (AI) teammates."*
- **Voice.** Plain, warm, confident, specific; no hype, no jargon. Write for someone who has never heard the word "agent."
- **Visual system.**
  - The hero visual is the product's **"Needs you" inbox** (approve / edit / skip) and the **weekly results report**. They show control and outcomes; avoid "robot" imagery.
  - No synthetic human faces in marketing (see §15).

## 4. Landing page

### 4.1 Objectives and stack
- **Pre-launch goal:** waitlist sign-up with segmentation. **Launch goal:** trial start.
- **Stack:**
  - A fast static site, so each page loads in under 1.5 seconds on mobile.
  - Product analytics and feature flags (PostHog; this repo already documents a PostHog connection in `doc/connections/POSTHOG.md`).
  - UTM conventions, a consent banner for EU/UK traffic, WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility, and server-side sign-up events.
- **Benchmarks:**
  - SaaS landing pages convert at a **3.8% median** (Unbounce, 41K pages) ([LanderLab](https://landerlab.io/blog/landing-page-conversion-rate)).
  - Self-serve pages should aim for 4–10%.
- **Our targets:** email-only waitlist 12–25% from warm social traffic and 5–8% from cold paid traffic; trial start ≥ 6% at launch.

### 4.2 Page architecture with draft copy

| # | Section | Draft copy and content | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|

[Brand]'s AI teammates handle follow-ups, proposals, content and admin, and check their work before it reaches you." CTA: **"Get early access"** (pre-launch) / **"Start your 14-day trial"** (launch). Visual: a 15-second loop of the "Needs you" inbox. | Clarity in 5 seconds |

| 6 | **Kits gallery** | 4 cards, each with its top 3 workflows and "see the kit" | Depth |

### 4.3 Industry kit pages (`/for/consultants`, `/for/coaches`, …)
- Built from a single template: industry-specific hero, the kit's workflows with example outputs, industry FAQs (for example compliance for coaches or agents), and an industry case study.
- These pages double as **SEO pages** ("AI for consultants" and similar) and as **ad destinations**. Industry-specific pages usually beat generic pages for paid traffic, and Phase 0 tests this directly.

### 4.4 Waitlist mechanics
- **Sign-up form:** email → then optional fields: industry, revenue band, and "top 3 jobs you'd hand off." This data feeds segmentation and interview recruiting, and becomes data for the State of OPC report.
- **Referral queue:** move up the list by inviting others. Rewards are early access, the founding-member price, and a free "kit setup call."
- **Founding members (first 100–300):** annual pre-pay at 40% off year one, plus a *Founding OPC* badge and a vote on the kit roadmap.
  - **No lifetime deals and no AppSumo:** AI lifetime deals carry open-ended inference cost, and the market is moving away from them ([ShareAI](https://shareai.now/blogs/insights/ai-lifetime-deal-pricing/), [Autoposting](https://autoposting.ai/blog/appsumo-review)).
- **Waitlist nurture:** biweekly "build in public" email, founder video updates, early kit previews, invitations to live office hours.

### 4.5 First six experiments (A/B or sequential)

| # | Test | Hypothesis | Metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "One-person company" framing vs "solo business" framing | The category term converts equal or better and builds the brand | Waitlist CVR |
| 2 | Generic hero vs industry-picker-first hero | Self-segmentation raises relevance | CVR and form completion |
| 3 | "Get early access" vs "Claim founding member spot" | Scarcity with a real limit lifts conversion | CVR, pre-pay rate |
| 4 | Static screenshot vs 15-second inbox loop | Motion showing control lifts trust | CVR, scroll depth |
| 5 | Price shown vs "pricing at launch" | Transparent pricing filters for real buyers | Pre-pay rate, later trial-to-paid |
| 6 | Kit page vs home page as the ad destination | Specific beats generic | Cost per waitlist sign-up |

### 4.6 Landing-page compliance checklist
- [ ] No income or earnings claims; all performance claims measured and documented (FTC substantiation).
- [ ] Testimonials and reviews are real, attributed and permissioned. No purchased, incentivized-but-undisclosed or AI-generated reviews. The FTC's reviews and testimonials rule (effective 21 Oct 2024) allows civil penalties up to **$51,744 per violation** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)).
- [ ] Any chat widget on the site discloses that it is AI (EU AI Act Article 50).
- [ ] Cookie consent (GDPR/ePrivacy); CAN-SPAM-compliant emails with a physical address and unsubscribe.
- [ ] Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA); fast on mobile.

## 6. Content strategy

### 6.1 The big idea: "Company of One, Run in Public"
The founder runs [Brand]'s own go-to-market **on [Brand]** as a real one-person company. Every Friday they publish the product's own **weekly results report**: what the team drafted, what was approved or rejected, what shipped, what it cost, and **what didn't work**.

- **Why it works:**
  - It demonstrates the product and the honesty positioning at the same time.
  - It builds in public, which was a core pillar of Lovable's playbook ([nrich](https://nrich.io/challenger-brand-gtm-library/lovable)).
  - It creates a weekly rhythm of content.
- **Rules:**
  - Real numbers only.
  - Disclose AI-drafted vs human-written content.
  - Never cherry-pick without saying so.

### 6.2 Content pillars

| Pillar | Share | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **OPC playbooks** (how to) | 35% | "The consultant's Friday follow-up system" · "One podcast episode → 12 posts, approved in 10 minutes" · kit walkthroughs |
| **Proof and teardowns** | 25% | Weekly results report · pilot case studies · "we tested 3 AI tools on a real proposal: here's what passed our definition of done" |
| **Category and data** | 15% | State of One-Person Companies stats (7.46M US nonemployers earn ≥$50K; industry cuts; the Korea and China OPC wave) · "what is a one-person company" |
| **Behind the build** | 15% | Plain-English explainers: "why our AI checks its own homework," "why we don't take a cut of your revenue," "how we keep AI costs fair" |
| **Community spotlights** | 10% | OPC member stories, Demo Day highlights |

### 6.3 Cadence and formats (founder + 1 editor; the Creator kit dogfoods the repurposing)

| Platform | Weekly cadence | Formats |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 1 long-form (8–15 min) + 3 Shorts | Kit walkthroughs, teardowns, weekly report readout |
| LinkedIn (founder) | 4 posts: 2 text, 1 document carousel, 1 short video; links go in *edits* or on profile, not in posts | Playbooks, data, weekly report |
| Instagram / TikTok | 4–5 Reels/TikToks + 1 carousel | "Watch it work" 20–40 second demos; before/after; persona POV |
| Threads / X | Daily short posts; 1 thread a week | Build in public, launch updates, harness story |
| Newsletter | 1 a week ("The OPC Weekly") | Weekly report, one playbook, one community story |
| Reddit | 2–3 helpful, non-promotional contributions a week; monthly AMA where allowed | Answer real questions; share data |
| Blog / SEO | 2 articles a week (pre-launch) → 3 a week | See §7 |

**Repurposing workflow:** one pillar asset per week (YouTube long-form or report) produces 10–15 derivative pieces. The product's own Creator kit does the repurposing, with the founder approving each piece. That is dogfooding plus content for the "run in public" series.

### 6.4 Hooks and templates (draft)

*All numbers in these templates are placeholders. Publish only real, measured figures (see §6.1 rules).*

- **LinkedIn text:** "I run a one-person company. Last week my AI team drafted 41 things. I approved 29, edited 8 and rejected 4. Here's the 4 it got wrong, and why that's the point ↓"
- **LinkedIn carousel:** "The 7 jobs every consultant should hand off first (and the 3 you never should)."
- **Short video (script, 30 seconds):** 0–3s "I gave my AI team one discovery call recording." 3–20s screen: transcript → proposal draft → checks passing (goals covered ✓, pricing matches ✓) → approve. 20–30s "It isn't done until it passes. I just hit send."
- **YouTube titles:** "I ran my business with an AI team for 30 days (the honest results)" · "AI for consultants: the follow-up system that runs itself (with approval)."
- **Reddit (r/consulting style, value first):** "I analyzed Census data on 1.08M solo consultants: only 26% earn over $50K. Here's the breakdown by industry and state" (share the data, with no link unless allowed).
- **X/Threads build-in-public:** "Week 6 running [Brand] as a one-person company: 3,112 waitlist, 12 pilot users, first-pass acceptance 64% (target 60%). Biggest miss: invoice reminders to the wrong contact. Fixed with a recipient check. Full report ↓"

### 6.5 AI-generated creative policy
- **No synthetic human presenters or testimonials.** Real founder and real customers only.
- **Label AI-generated visuals:**
  - Meta auto-labels content that carries C2PA provenance data; declare AI use at ad setup.
  - TikTok requires an "AI-generated" disclosure toggle for ads.
  - YouTube requires disclosure of realistic altered or synthetic content ([Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/ai-disclosure-rules/), [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-content-labeling-rules-advertisers-2026-reference)).
- New York has required disclosure of "synthetic performers" in ads since **9 June 2026**, and the EU AI Act Article 50 marking duties have applied since **2 August 2026**.

## 8. Community: the OPC Club

- **Why:** China's OPC wave shows that *belonging* (OPC communities, peer groups) matters as much as tools. No Western platform owns this.
- **Format:**
  - A free community space (Circle, Skool or Discord), open to non-customers.
  - Channels for each industry.
  - Weekly live **Office Hours** (founder plus a guest).
  - Monthly **OPC Demo Day**: members show their weekly reports and kits.
  - A 30-day **"OPC Sprint"** challenge each quarter, with public progress.
- **Share loop:**
  - Opt-in public **company pages** ("Maya Consulting: a one-person company run on [Brand]") showing selected weekly outcomes.
  - A "Run your company like this" button clones the kit, which is the equivalent of Lovable's "Edit with Lovable" loop ([Startup Riders](https://www.startupriders.com/p/how-lovable-hit-400m-arr-in-14-months)).
- **Rules:** no income claims or "get rich" posts; real results only; moderators from the member base.
- **KPIs:** weekly active members; members who start a trial; Demo Day attendance; user-generated kit submissions (Phase 2 marketplace).

## 12. Paid acquisition (gated tests)

**Only start paid when:** landing-to-trial ≥ 6%, activation ≥ 50%, and trial-to-paid ≥ 15%. Run the first tests at **$10–12K total** across channels for 4–6 weeks.

| Channel | 2026 cost benchmarks | Test design | Kill or scale rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meta** (Reels, Feed) | CPM ≈ $14 all-industry average; CPC ≈ $0.78 (traffic) to ~$1.70 blended ([ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/meta-ads-benchmarks)) | Industry creatives → kit pages; lookalikes of paying users; 10–20 second "watch it work" demos | Kill if CAC > $250 after $2K per ad set; scale +20%/week while CAC ≤ $200 |
| **YouTube** (in-feed, Shorts) | CPV ≈ $0.05–0.15; CPM $6–12 ([LocaliQ](https://localiq.com/blog/youtube-advertising-cost/)) | Promote the best-performing organic tutorials | Same |
| **Google Search** | Varies by keyword | High intent: "AI assistant for consultants," "proposal software for consultants," honest competitor comparisons | CAC ≤ $200 |
| **LinkedIn** | CPC ≈ $5–10 ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/linkedin-ads-benchmarks-cpc-ctr-2026)) | Small test: boost founder posts as "thought leader ads" to consultants | Expect a high CPL; keep only if trial-to-paid is exceptional |
| **Reddit** | — | Niche subreddits, with offer transparency | CAC ≤ $200 |

**Creative principles:**
- Show the "magic moment" in the first 3 seconds: a proposal drafted and *checked* in under a minute. That is the Lovable pattern of short demos as ads.
- Founder to camera; real customers.
- Every claim substantiated. No synthetic people.

## 13. Conversion, onboarding and lifecycle

### 13.1 Trial design
- **Launch:** a 14-day trial with **no card required**, to get volume and learning, but capped at ~30 jobs to contain inference cost.
- **Benchmarks:**
  - Trials without a card convert **8–22% (median ~14%)**.
  - Trials that require a card convert **35–55% (median ~44%)**, but bring in 30–50% less volume ([shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/free-trial-conversion-statistics)).
- **Test a card-required variant** after W+4, once onboarding quality is proven.
- **Activation definition:** the first *accepted* deliverable within 10 minutes, plus 3 accepted deliverables in week 1. Research on activation shows a 25% activation lift can raise monthly retention by ~34% ([ProductLed](https://productled.com/blog/activation-rate-saas)).

### 13.2 Onboarding sequence (in-app plus email)

| Day | Trigger | Message / action |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Sign-up | 5-minute business interview → kit selected → first job runs live ("watch it work") |
| 0 | First approval | 🎉 "Your first finished job." Show the definition-of-done checks that passed |
| 1 | No approval yet | "Your team drafted 3 things. Approve, edit or skip in 60 seconds" (mobile deep link) |
| 3 | Connect accounts | "Connect Gmail/Calendar so follow-ups run on their own (with your OK)" |
| 5 | First Friday | **Weekly results report**, the "aha" email: hours saved, jobs done, what didn't work |
| 7 | Mid-trial | Kit tips from a creator in their industry; invite to Office Hours |
| 10 | Pre-decision | "Here's what your team finished so far" plus plan recommendation from actual usage |
| 13 | Last day | Honest summary; founding price if eligible; export option if they leave |

### 13.3 Retention and virality
- **Weekly results report** is the retention engine and the share unit: one-tap share as an image or link, with "Run on [Brand]".
- **Referral:** double-sided, one free month each, offered after a *positive moment* (for example an approved streak or a good weekly report). Well-run SaaS referral programs see 10–15% of active users take part, and referred users convert better ([GrowSurf](https://growsurf.com/statistics/saas-referral-statistics/)).
- **Email benchmarks:** judge emails by clicks (average ~2–2.5%), not opens; Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates open rates ([ClickMinded](https://www.clickminded.com/email-marketing-benchmarks/)).
- **Win-back:** 30 days after churn, send "what changed," covering new kit workflows and acceptance-rate improvements.

## 15. Compliance and risk (marketing)

| Risk | Rule / control |
|---|---|
