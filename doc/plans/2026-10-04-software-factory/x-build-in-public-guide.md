## Part 1. X in 10 minutes (glossary for beginners)

| Term | What it means | Why you care |
|---|---|---|
| **Post** | A message of up to 280 characters (longer on paid plans) with optional images, video or a poll | Your basic unit |
| **Reply** | A post under someone else's post | Your #1 growth tool when you're new |
| **Repost / Quote** | Sharing someone's post; *quote* adds your comment on top | Quotes put your take in front of your followers |
| **Thread** | Several posts chained together (you reply to your own post) | Best for engineering stories and research |
| **For You** | The algorithm feed: posts X thinks a person will like, including from accounts they don't follow | Where new followers find you |
| **Following** | A time-ordered feed of accounts a person follows | Where existing followers see you |
| **Bookmark** | A private save | A strong signal of value; technical threads earn lots of them |
| **Communities** | Topic groups (for example "Build in Public") that anyone can post into | Reach an interested crowd with zero followers |
| **Lists** | Your own curated feeds of accounts | Your daily "who to reply to" list |
| **Spaces** | Live audio rooms | Launch-week AMAs and "Lights-out Friday" streams |
| **Pinned post** | The post fixed at the top of your profile | Your permanent introduction |
| **Premium (blue check)** | Paid subscription | Reply visibility and reach (Part 2) |
| **Impressions / engagement rate** | Times shown / interactions ÷ impressions | Reach / quality |

### How the For You feed ranks posts (2026, simplified)

X has published much of its ranking code. Third-party analyses agree on the direction, though not on exact numbers ([Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/twitter-algorithm/), [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/how-the-x-twitter-algorithm-works), [Postory](https://postory.io/blog/x-algorithm-2026)).
- **Conversations beat likes.** Replies, especially ones you answer, and bookmarks, profile clicks and reposts all count far more than likes.
- **Replies are judged on substance.** Thoughtful replies rank above empty ones.
- **Links cost reach.** An external link in the main post reduces initial reach. Put GitHub and blog links in a reply.
- **Topic consistency helps.** Stay on one theme: AI software factories and building with agents.
- **Premium accounts get a boost,** most visibly in reply ranking.
- **Freshness matters.** Most reach happens in the first hours.

**What this means for you:** one clear theme, real conversations, links in replies, and showing up daily.

## Part 2. Set up your account (day 1, about 90 minutes)

### 2.1 Personal account, not a brand account

Engineers follow engineers. Use **your own name**. Create `@[Brand]` later for release notes and status. Your personal account does the building in public.

### 2.2 Settings checklist

- [ ] **Username:** your real name, or name plus a short word (`@samships`, `@alexbuilds`). Under 15 characters; avoid numbers.
- [ ] **Display name:** `[First Last] · building [Brand]`. Before the name is public, use `[First Last] · building an AI software factory`.
- [ ] **Profile photo:** a clear face photo; no logo.
- [ ] **Header (1500×500):** "Building an AI software factory, built by its own factory," plus "Day 1 → public beta Jan 2027." Update it as you go.
- [ ] **Bio (160 characters).** Pick one:
  - "Building [Brand]: an AI software factory that proves every change. It builds itself; I post the weekly numbers, failures included. Ex-[role]."
  - "Engineer running a software factory. Specs in, verified software out. Weekly Factory Report. Day [N]."
- [ ] **Website:** the waitlist page once live; later your GitHub organization.
- [ ] **Security:**
  - two-factor authentication with an authenticator app or security key, never SMS;
  - a strong, unique password;
  - review connected apps.
  
  Developer-founders are frequent phishing targets.
- [ ] **Notifications:** replies and mentions only.

## Part 3. Find your people before you post much (days 1–3)

You're writing for **three audiences**:
1. **Builders on agents:** engineers using Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and similar tools daily. They are your peers and your toughest critics.
2. **Technical founders and indie hackers:** people running SaaS products alone or in tiny teams. They are your first customers.
3. **Agency owners and freelancers:** people paid per delivery. They are your highest-value early customers.

### 3.1 Build three private lists

| List | Who goes on it | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily 25** | Mid-sized accounts (5K–150K followers) posting daily about AI coding, agents, developer tools or building in public, with real reply threads | 25 |
| **Customers** | Solo founders, small-team CTOs and agency owners who post about shipping and maintaining their products | 50–100 |
| **Peers** | Other founders at your stage (under 5K followers) building developer tools in public | 30–50 |

**How to find them:**
- Search for `"Claude Code"`, `"coding agent"`, `"agentic coding"`, `"vibe coding"`, `"software factory"`, `"indie hacker"`, `"solo founder"`, `"dev agency"` and `"build in public"`.
- Use the **Latest** tab and watch who gets good replies.
- Look at who replies well under AI-lab, developer-tool and indie-founder accounts.
- Join the **Build in Public** community and one AI-engineering or indie-hacker community.

### 3.2 Follow 100–200 relevant accounts

Follow only accounts in your topics. It teaches the algorithm your theme and fills your For You feed with reply opportunities.

## Part 4. Your story and positioning

Write this once; reuse it everywhere.

- **Who you are:** "I'm [you]. I'm building an AI software factory, and the factory is building itself."
- **The problem you've seen:**
  - agents write code fast, but someone still has to review it, test it, secure it, pay for its mistakes, and keep it away from production;
  - small teams have no platform team to do that.
- **What you believe:** "Engineers will stop typing code and start running factories." **Present this as a belief.** You'll show the evidence week by week. It is not a promise.
- **What you're doing:**
  - building [Brand] with [Brand];
  - publishing the weekly Factory Report from your own repository;
  - building a public benchmark of what a *verified* change really costs.
- **What you'll share:** real numbers, failures, engineering decisions, research and every weekly report.
- **What you won't do:**
  - claim "it replaces engineers";
  - post unmeasured multiples;
  - post customer code;
  - dunk on competitors.

**Your recurring series:**

| Series | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Factory Report** | Every Friday | Changes merged by line, first-pass merge rate, false-green audit, cost per change, lights-out share, **what failed and why** |
| **Factory Data** | 1–2× a week | A stat plus your take, from the research (METR horizons, DORA, review burden, cost per change) |
| **Build Log** | 2–3× a week | A screenshot or short screen recording (evidence card, inbox, X-ray) plus the decision behind it |
| **Partner Voice** | 1× a week | A lesson from a design partner (anonymized, with permission) |
| **Ask** | 1× a week | A question or poll for engineers and founders |

## Part 5. What to post: five content pillars

These fit [marketing plan §6.2](./marketing-plan.md#62-content-pillars), tuned for X.

| Pillar | Share | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Factory floor (proof)** | 30% | Factory Report · "a bug from issue to verified PR in 11 minutes" (real recording) · a lights-out promotion with its evidence |
| **2. How we verify (engineering)** | 25% | Holdout scenarios vs test gaming · why build agents can't merge · order sizing from METR horizons · the false-green audit |
| **3. Research and data** | 20% | "656 negative reviews of AI coding tools: two-thirds are about money" · benchmark results · cost per verified change |
| **4. Partner stories and questions** | 15% | Design-partner lessons; polls ("What would you let merge without you?") |
| **5. Founder journey and values** | 10% | The pivot, pricing decisions, bad weeks, what you refuse to build |

### 5.1 Post formats that work for developer audiences

| Format | Why it works | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Short text post** (1–4 lines) | Easy to reply to | End with a question or a clear, defensible opinion |
| **Screenshot + one line** | Real work beats claims | Crop tightly; blur repository names and secrets |
| **Short screen recording** (10–40 s) | Watching an order go green is the "aha" | Record real runs, failures included; no music needed |
| **Thread** (5–8 posts) | Engineering stories earn bookmarks | The first post must stand alone; link the code or method in the last reply |
| **Chart or table image** | Data travels | Always show the source and method |
| **Code snippet image** | Developers love concrete config | Show the line spec YAML or an order contract, never customer code |
| **Poll** | Cheap research | Use for discovery: autonomy thresholds, pricing, lines |

### 5.2 How to write a post that engineers read

1. **Hook with a number or a surprising result.** For example: "Our agents 'fixed' 7 failing tests this week by editing the tests."
2. **One idea per post.**
3. **Precise words:** "first-pass merge rate," "false green," not "amazing results."
4. **Show the method,** or link it in a reply. Engineers check.
5. **Admit limits** before someone else points them out.
6. **Links go in a reply.**
7. **Zero or one hashtag.**
8. **Read it out loud.** If it sounds like a launch ad, rewrite it.

## Part 6. Grow with replies (your main tool for months 1–2)

With zero followers, your own posts reach almost nobody. **Replies put you in front of other people's audiences.** Founder playbooks suggest about 20 good replies a day plus a few original posts ([Innmind](https://blog.innmind.com/x-growth-playbook-for-startup-founders-2026/), [FounderDistro](https://www.founderdistro.com/blog/build-in-public-x-twitter-guide)).

### 6.1 The reply routine

- Open your **Daily 25** list twice a day. Reply within 30–60 minutes of the post.
- Add something:
  - **Data:** "Related: METR's best agent finishes about 3 hours of expert work reliably (80%) — that's why we split work into orders."
  - **Experience:** "We saw the same; holdout scenarios stopped our agents from editing tests to pass."
  - **A respectful counterpoint:** "Agree for greenfield code; on legacy repositories, review time is the real bottleneck."
  - **A good question:** "How do you decide what an agent can merge without you?"
- Never write "great post," never drop your link, and never pitch unless asked.

### 6.2 Reply quality ladder

| Level | Example | Value |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Empty | "This 🔥" | Hurts you |
| ⚠️ Agreeing | "100% agree." | Near zero |
| ✅ Adding | "Agree. Our partners' biggest time sink isn't writing code, it's reviewing it." | Good |
| ⭐ Mini-post | 2–3 lines with a number, a failure story or a method that stands on its own | Best: earns follows |

### 6.3 Replying to your own replies

Answer every reply within a few hours, ideally within the first hour. Conversation depth is a strong ranking signal, and engineers respect founders who engage with criticism.

### 6.4 Direct messages

- Build relationships; never pitch cold.
- Good reasons to DM:
  - thank someone who shared your work;
  - ask a follow-up question ("Could I ask 3 questions about how you handle dependency upgrades?");
  - invite someone as a design partner, after you've talked in public.

## Part 7. Build-in-public rules: what to share and what not to

| Share ✅ | Don't share ❌ |
|---|---|
| Your own repository's Factory Report numbers | **Customer code, repository names, issue text or secrets** without written permission |
| What you built and why; architecture at a high level | Security internals that would help an attacker (sandbox escape details, egress rules) |
| Failures, false greens and postmortems | Unpatched vulnerabilities in anyone's software (report privately first) |
| Research with sources and methods | Unverified or cherry-picked numbers presented as fact |
| Costs ("cost per merged Medium change: $4.80 this week") | "Replaces engineers" or unmeasured multiples as product claims |
| Decisions and trade-offs | NDA material; investor terms before they're public |

**Honesty rule:** when a number is bad, post it anyway, with what you're changing. That's the brand.

## Part 8. Measure what matters

Check X Analytics once a week, on Friday, for 15 minutes.

| Metric | Early target (first 90 days) | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Followers | 500 by day 45 · **1,500–3,000 by launch** | Reach is building |
| Replies per post (median) | 3+ | People want to talk to you |
| Profile visits → follows | ≥ 5% | Profile and pinned post convert |
| Bookmarks per thread | 15+ | Your engineering content is useful |
| **Waitlist sign-ups from X** (UTM `?utm_source=x`) | 30% of the waitlist | X is doing its job |
| Design-partner and customer DMs | 5+ a week by month 2 | Real demand |

Keep a spreadsheet: date, post, format, impressions, replies, bookmarks, follows, sign-ups. Every Friday, note one thing to keep and one to stop.

**Realistic expectations:** slow for 4–8 weeks, then faster. One great reply under a big AI-engineering account can beat a week of your own posts.

## Part 9. Your daily routine (about 60–75 minutes)

| Time | Task | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Daily 25 list → 8–10 good replies | 25 |
| Morning | Publish the post of the day (drafted the night before) | 5 |
| Midday | Answer every reply; 5 replies on the Customers list | 15 |
| Evening | 5–7 replies on Peers and communities; DMs | 15 |
| Evening | Draft tomorrow's post (Part 11) | 10 |

**Weekly:**
- **Monday:** plan 5–7 posts.
- **Wednesday:** one engineering thread.
- **Friday:** the Factory Report plus analytics.
- **Sunday:** rest or draft only.

**Posting times:** start with 8–10 a.m. and 12–2 p.m. US Eastern (much of the developer audience is US and EU). After 4 weeks, follow your analytics.

**Tools:** the native X scheduler or Typefully for drafts; your phone or a screen recorder for short videos; the factory itself to produce the Factory Report numbers (dogfooding).

## Part 12. Rules, safety and etiquette

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **No unmeasured claims** ("10x," "bug-free," "replaces engineers") as product claims | FTC substantiation; credibility with engineers ([marketing plan §2.3](./marketing-plan.md#23-language-rules-brand-legal-and-trust)) |
| **Never post customer code, repository names, issue text or secrets** without written permission; blur by default | Security and trust |
| **Report vulnerabilities privately first** (responsible disclosure); never as content | Ethics; legal risk |
| **Disclose relationships** (sponsors, partners, paid content) | FTC Endorsement Guides |
| **Real testimonials only,** with permission | FTC reviews rule (penalties up to $51,744 per violation) |
| **No engagement bots, bought followers or automated DMs** | Against X's rules |
| **Criticize products, not people;** state competitors' strengths fairly | Reputation; engineers notice unfairness |
| **Keep company secrets private:** keys, internal URLs, sandbox internals | Security |
| **Take breaks;** mute and block freely; don't argue with trolls | Sustainability |

### Common beginner mistakes (developer-tool edition)

1. Posting links in every post.
2. Posting only product updates. People follow people and engineering stories.
3. Hiding failures. Engineers find them anyway.
4. Chasing viral "AI will replace devs" takes. You'll attract the wrong audience and the wrong fights.
5. Checking analytics hourly.
6. Waiting to be "ready." Start with replies today.
